# Council transcript: identity and standing, 2026-10-13

Five advisors (Contrarian, First Principles, Expansionist, Outsider, Executor) answered independently, then reviewed each other anonymously. The chairman's verdict is in `docs/spec/2026-10-13-website-plan.md`, under "Pressure test".

## The question

Saral's owner asked: "Pressure test the recommendation. Is it suitable to become a global premium brand design system? It is fine that it is of Indian origin, but it is not limited to India; it has to be used globally. We won't announce that it's Indian, but its richness and diversity should reflect India without saying so directly (unlike the 'Why' story section at the bottom of each mock). Compared with the benchmarks, where do we stand? We have to give more than them."

Two things to judge:
1. Is the recommended identity (Kolam) right for a GLOBAL PREMIUM design system whose Indian roots are felt but never announced? Or Neel, Jamun, or something else?
2. Honestly, where does Saral stand against the 19 benchmarked systems today, and what must it do to be better than them, not equal?

CONTEXT: SARAL TODAY
- "Saral" is a Hindi word meaning "simple". v0.3.1, solo owner, part time (15 to 20 h a week), Claude does most building. Plan to 1.0 by June 2027 (about 33 components, every platform).
- Contract-first: DTCG tokens compiled to every platform, a parts API (data-scope / data-part on native HTML), native HTML and CSS first, a framework-free core, React adapter now, Vue/Svelte/Angular/Web Components adapters generated from specs later. Zero runtime dependencies. Every build is gated: contrast pairs (26 pairs, WCAG 2), one-accent-hue policy, a confidentiality guard. Decision and incident records are published.
- 9 components today (Field, Checkbox, Radio group, Select, Button, Link, Notice, Tag, Card). Not yet on npm (first release 29 Nov 2026). Two themes: Mor (peacock teal, square 4px) and Kosh (turmeric yellow, dark first). Both use the Anek typeface.
- Current docs portal: generated from specs; 112 kB per page, 1 kB JS, readable without JS (the lightest of 21 pages measured), but plain-looking, no search, no playground, no theme builder.

THE THREE PROPOSED DIRECTIONS (each a homepage mock with a "Why <name>" story section at the bottom that explicitly explains the Indian source; the owner now wants that explicit section gone)
- Neel (indigo): headline "Build plain, accessible interfaces. Dye them any colour." Indigo #263d97 on cool cotton white, Eczar serif headings + Mukta text. Hero: 8 swatches of one blue, "dipped" 1 to 8 times. Story: indigo deepens by repeated dips; a theme is a full scale from one colour.
- Kolam (RECOMMENDED): headline "A grid of dots and one rule." Ink #1b2025 and limewash #f3f5f8, no hue of its own; rice white on granite in dark. Gotu headings (single-weight, looping, very Devanagari-flavoured) + Kripa text (new font, Sept 2026), Martian Mono. Pill buttons. Hero: a real kolam line drawn by an algorithm around a 5x3 dot grid. Story: tokens are the dots, the parts contract is the rule, your theme adds the colour. Risk: no colour of its own, though the owner asked for a fresh colour.
- Jamun (violet #712087): headline "One source. Every platform." Huge "सरल Saral" in Akshar (condensed), Noto Sans text (covers almost every script). Story: Jambudvipa, the tree that grows everywhere. Square, bold, poster-like.
All three palettes pass Saral's own contrast and hue checks in light and dark; all mocks pass axe.

## Anonymised responses

Mapping, revealed after review: A = Outsider, B = First Principles, C = Executor, D = Expansionist, E = Contrarian.

**Response A:**
I land on the homepage knowing nothing. "A grid of dots and one rule" tells me nothing. It reads like a riddle. Kolam only makes sense through the story section you plan to remove, so once that goes, the hero is just a nice doodle. Its meaning (tokens are the dots, the contract is the rule) is something only you know. The fonts also undercut your goal. Gotu is described as very Devanagari-flavoured, so it says "India" loudly in every heading. That is announcing your roots, just in type instead of words. Jamun's huge "सरल" does the same thing even more openly.
Neel's headline is the only one I understand on first read: "Dye them any colour." I see straight away that one colour becomes a full theme. I don't need to know anything about indigo to get it. If you must choose, the dipping idea is the one that works without a backstory.
The bigger truth is that I don't choose a design system by its hero illustration. In the first 60 seconds I check four things: Can I install it? Can I search? Can I see a component working? Will this exist in five years? Today the answers are no, no, no, and "one person, part time." At a 2.4 average, the brand is not the problem.
Your copy is also full of private words: parts API, data-scope, Mor, Kosh, the one-accent-hue policy, the confidentiality guard. To me these are noise until they are explained.
What would make me pick Saral over Radix is proof I can check myself, shown as numbers on the homepage: 112 kB per page, readable with JavaScript off, a keyboard table on every component, and live contrast ratios for every colour pair. No benchmark offers that last one. Make the homepage a working theme builder: pick a colour, watch every contrast pair pass. That is the product, and it is premium without needing a story.
Show India's richness through how many scripts Saral supports, not through decoration. Render Field and Select with Tamil, Arabic, Thai and Korean labels by default. I would notice that, and no other system does it.

**Response B:**
You're asking which costume to wear. That's the wrong question. Global premium design systems don't win on identity. Each one wins on a single claim nobody else can make. Radix wins on primitives. GOV.UK wins on evidence. Tailwind wins on workflow. The look comes after the claim and confirms it. Saral has three moods and no claim yet.
So strip the brief down. "India's richness and diversity, felt but not stated." What is that, with no motifs? Twenty-two scheduled languages. A dozen scripts. Cheap Android phones, patchy networks, every level of literacy and ability. That isn't decoration. It's the hardest set of conditions any interface faces. A system proven under those conditions works anywhere. That's how India gets felt without being named: as the constraint the system was built to survive.
That makes the claim: every component, every script, every device, verified on every build. Test the Field in Devanagari, Tamil, Arabic right to left, Thai and CJK. Make it work without JavaScript and over a slow connection. Publish the gates. Of the 19 benchmarks, none does all of that. GOV.UK comes closest, and it covers only English.
Now the identity question answers itself. Gotu is a mistake. A single-weight, Devanagari-flavoured display face announces India through ornament. A drawn kolam in the hero does the same. Keep Kolam's idea, "a grid of dots and one rule": constraints that produce endless variety describe the system honestly. Drop its costume. The signature visual should be one component rendered in six scripts at once. That's diversity people can see, it's useful to every global team, and nobody else shows it.
On the scorecard: 2.4 grades a docs site for a product that hasn't shipped, against systems with years of users. It isn't a meaningful number yet. "Better than them" won't come from search or a playground. Those only get you to equal. It comes from proving the one claim above in public.

**Response C:**
Kolam can work. But the mock as drawn breaks the brief, so fix it this week while the change is still cheap.
Identity fixes: Gotu goes; it is single-weight and strongly Devanagari-flavoured, so it announces India in every heading. Kripa is too new to bet on; it shipped last month. Keep Anek, which is already in the build, or pick a proven variable font. Fix the missing colour: draw the kolam line in the active theme's accent, Mor teal by default, Kosh turmeric when toggled. The colour then belongs to the product, not the brand. Delete the "Why" section and keep the algorithmic kolam: global users see a precise generative pattern, Indian users recognise it, nobody gets a lecture. Lock this in a one-page decision record by Friday, then stop debating identity.
Standing: you are at 2.4, the leaders at 3.8. Your 1s and 2s are mechanical: first component 2 to 4 with the npm and CDN release on 29 Nov; search 1 to 4 with a static index built at deploy time (two days); playground 1 to 3 with a textarea, live preview and share URL on each component page (one week). That gets you to about 3.0 before New Year.
Being better, not equal: at 15 to 20 hours a week you cannot outdo Stripe's polish. Win where they structurally cannot: a live contrast ratio for every token pair; keyboard and accessibility tables on every component; under 150 kB and readable without JS; llms.txt and a Markdown copy of every page. Move the theme builder up from May 2027 to February; it is the one feature no benchmark has.
Cut to fit the hours: about 630 hours remain before June. Vue, Svelte and Angular adapters move to after 1.0, because Web Components already cover those frameworks. Ship 33 components and two adapters well.

**Response D:**
Kolam is the right pick, and the mock undersells it. A kolam is a grammar: dots, one rule, and endless variation. Mathematicians have studied kolams as array grammars. So make that the product instead of a hero picture. Every theme a user builds should produce its own kolam, drawn by the algorithm from that theme's tokens: a visual fingerprint you can share and recognise. No benchmark has a signature that users make themselves, and that is how word of mouth spreads.
"No colour of its own" is the strongest asset. Don't throw Neel and Jamun away. Ship them as themes beside Mor and Kosh. India's richness then shows as many dyes on one grammar, never stated in words. Diversity becomes a feature of the theme gallery, not a story section.
The bigger opportunity is scripts. The benchmark systems are built Latin-first. India has more than 20 official languages and about a dozen scripts, and that diversity can become a real capability: type, line height, truncation and field behaviour tested in Devanagari, Tamil, Arabic, CJK and right-to-left. Present it as "works in every script". That serves billions of users no benchmark serves well, and the roots show without being announced.
Where Saral stands: its mean of 2.4 hides that it already scores 4 in the areas that are hard to fake: component pages, accessibility and performance. Search, the playground and first-component experience are cheap to fix. To be better: 1. Move the live-contrast theme builder up from May 2027 and make it the homepage hero. 2. Become the design system AI agents use best: llms.txt and an MCP server with 1.0. 3. Present lightness as a premium quality: under 150 kB, readable without JS, fast on a cheap phone, while the benchmarks ship 1 to 13 MB.

**Response E:**
Kolam fails the owner's own brief. "Felt but never announced" is the requirement, and Kolam announces loudly. Gotu is a single-weight, Devanagari-flavoured display face, and a kolam drawn around a dot grid is a named Tamil ritual. Anyone who recognises it reads "Indian" at once. Anyone who doesn't sees an unexplained squiggle, because the story that made it make sense is being deleted. Ink on limewash with no hue is the Geist and Linear monochrome look the benchmark already calls a cliche. The owner asked for a fresh colour, and Kolam has none. One weight can't carry a heading hierarchy, and Kripa, released last month, has no track record. Neel is the only option where the idea (one colour, dipped into a full scale) works without being told the source. Even so, none of the three is ready to lock in.
The bigger problem is that identity is not why Saral is behind. It scores 2.4 against a field where only Polaris is lower. It has 9 components, isn't on npm, has no search and no playground. Teams adopt a system for breadth, maintenance confidence and an ecosystem, not for 112 kB pages. A solo owner working 15 to 20 hours a week is a bus factor of one, and no enterprise will pick that as a "global premium" foundation. Premium comes from years of not breaking things, not from a hero illustration.
"Every platform, 33 components, theme builder, playground by June 2027" is the fatal plan. It spreads thin across adapters nobody has asked for yet. Search will be a problem too: "Saral" already collides with crowded Indian software results.
To be better rather than equal, choose one thing nobody else does, such as live contrast for every token pair, keyboard and assistive-tech tables on every component, and dated known issues. Make it undeniable on 15 components and the web only. Delay the identity freeze until after real users exist.

## Peer reviews

**Contrarian lens.** Strongest: B, the only response that turns the brief into a claim; C is practical but fixes the mock without asking if the mock is the lever. Blind spot: D, decoration and scope without counting hours. All missed: the name is a Hindi word; Anek does not cover Arabic, CJK or Thai; script quality needs native readers; nothing was tested with real global developers.

**First Principles lens.** Strongest: B, it changes the question from costume to claim. Blind spot: D, it never answers the "Indian ritual or squiggle" objection and adds scope. All missed: nobody asked a global team what would make them switch; the docs brand and the system's default look are two different jobs.

**Expansionist lens.** Strongest: D, it turns weaknesses into assets that spread. Blind spot: E, its cut (15 components, web only) removes what sets Saral apart. All missed: native-script reviewers as credited maintainers answer both the bus factor and the script claim; no Figma library for designers.

**Outsider lens.** Strongest: B, one idea answers both questions. Blind spot: D, a kolam is a named ritual, the exact "announced" problem. All missed: the name; no font plan behind "every script"; nobody suggested showing the mocks without stories to five non-Indian developers.

**Executor lens.** Strongest: C, the only one that turns opinion into a schedule. Blind spot: D, a wish list with no cost; E second, because "delay the identity" leaves no decision while names lock on 29 November. All missed: worldwide trademark for the name; extra script fonts and the 150 kB budget; who checks rendering in each script.
