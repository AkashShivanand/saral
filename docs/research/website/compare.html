Neel indigo

Calm, crafted, editorial. Light first.

Open the full mock

Story, for you only

Indigo takes its name from the Greek indikon, from India. In the vat the dye is not blue at all; it is yellow-green. Cloth comes out green and turns blue in the air. A dyer gets a deeper blue by dipping again, not by making the vat stronger. Saral works the same way. One colour, applied by one simple process, gives a whole scale. A theme is a few repeated, patient steps, and every step is checked before the next. Sources: Botanical Colors, indigo FAQ, DGNHAS, Indigo: a blue to dye for.

Type

Eczar for headings (Rosetta, Latin and Devanagari, variable weight). Mukta for text (Ek Type, Latin and Devanagari). JetBrains Mono for code.

Kolam the threshold drawing

Precise, geometric, quietly playful. Ink and rice, no hue of its own.

Open the full mock

Story, for you only

Every morning, across South India, a kolam is drawn at the threshold of a home. It starts as a grid of dots, the pulli. A line of rice flour then loops around the dots by a few simple rules, and the same grid gives endless designs. The daily kolam is white only; colour is saved for festivals. Saral is built like this. Tokens are the dots, the parts contract is the rule, and your theme is the festival colour. The drawing above is real: one line, one rule, a five by three grid. Sources: Kolam, Wikipedia.

Type

Gotu for headings (Ek Type, a single-weight line that loops like a kolam stroke). Kripa for text (Kedar Gadge, variable weight, released on Google Fonts in September 2026). Martian Mono for code.

Jamun the black plum

Bold, contemporary, confident. A full-colour field and poster type.

Open the full mock

Story, for you only

One of the oldest names for India is Jambudvipa, the land of the jambu tree, the jamun. Ashoka used it for his realm in the third century BCE. The jamun grows in almost any soil, belongs to nobody in particular, and feeds whoever passes under it. Its colour stays on everything it touches. That is the promise of Saral: one source that grows on every platform, free for anyone to use, and a mark that stays consistent wherever it lands. Sources: Jambudvipa, Wikipedia.

Type

Akshar for headings (Tall Chai, condensed, variable weight and contrast). Noto Sans with Noto Sans Devanagari for text: the one free family that covers nearly every script, so Tamil, Bengali or Urdu can follow without a new font. Noto Sans Mono for code.
