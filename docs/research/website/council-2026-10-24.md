# Council, 24 October 2026: which look represents Saral

A blind pressure test of all fifteen website directions, run on 2026-10-24 at the owner's request ("review all these design options as design director and creative director, without any bias"). Five advisors (Contrarian, First Principles, Expansionist, Outsider, Executor) judged the options as letters A to O in random order, with no source, no earlier scores and no earlier recommendation. Five peer reviewers then judged the five answers anonymously. A separate chairman wrote the verdict.

Key to the letters (not shown to the council): A Editorial Signal (ChatGPT), B Architectural Blueprint (Gemini), C The Chameleon (Gemini), D Adaptive Prism (ChatGPT), E The Translator (Gemini), F Modular Architecture (ChatGPT), G Living Material (ChatGPT), H Soft Contrast (Gemini), I Plain sheet, J Neel, K Kolam, L Two brands, M Bare Bones (Gemini), N Spec sheet, O Script & Signal (ChatGPT).

Known limit: options I to N were rendered HTML mock-ups and the others AI-generated pictures, so finish differed; two reviewers flagged that every top three came from the rendered set. The chairman judged the verdict holds because the others lose on measured contrast and on the hard rules.


## The question put to the council

Which visual direction (or which combination) should represent Saral, so that its website and docs could stand beside the best design systems in the world (Radix, Stripe docs, GOV.UK Design System, Tailwind, Material, Carbon)? Judge all fifteen options fairly, as a design director and a creative director would.

## What Saral is

An open-source design system: themeable tokens and native HTML components (real button, input, select) that work in any stack, in any script including right to left, and before JavaScript loads. Nine components today, about twenty plus form patterns by 1.0. Its job is forms and services that work for everyone. Headline under consideration: "Build it plain. Make it yours."

Audience: front-end developers, product designers, and teams building public services and financial products worldwide. Two very different websites with their own brand guidelines will use it first. One site holds the landing page and the docs; a lab section holds the playground and live case studies.

## Hard constraints the chosen look must live with

- The website is styled only by Saral's own tokens and components. Components use semantic tokens only; a theme may use one accent hue (status colours aside).
- Every page reads with JavaScript off and works in forced colours (Windows High Contrast).
- First load at or under 150 kB including fonts.
- Text must render well in Latin, Devanagari, Tamil, Arabic (right to left), Thai and Japanese.
- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast in light and dark.
- Plain words, sentence case, no hype; no business or promotional content; never name the sites that use it.
- The founder is Indian. The owner ruled that Indian roots should be felt in richness and range, never announced through ornament or explained on the site. The system is meant to be global.

## What is at stake

The look chosen now becomes a real Saral theme that ships with the docs site in February 2027 and shapes how every team first judges the system. Changing it after 1.0 is costly.

## Measured facts given to the chairman

These are measurements, not opinions. They answer some blind spots the reviewers raised.

- **J's contrast result used a stated dark palette.** Option J's file gives dark-mode colours, and its 9 of 9 includes three dark pairs from them. It was not an inverted palette. Options L, N, I and K were checked in dark mode as plain inverses because they give no dark palette.
- **A spike was built for J and N.** Each became a real Button docs page made from the system's own CSS and a full set of 33 colour roles in light and dark (26 contrast pairs each, all passing, one accent hue). N's build used the system's own sans (Anek, which covers Latin, Devanagari and Tamil) in place of Geist, and the system monospace for code. J's build used Eczar (a serif with Latin and Devanagari) for headings and Anek for text, in place of Newsreader.
- **Results of that spike, both pages:** axe (WCAG 2.2 AA tags) clean in light and dark at 1280 and 375 pixels; no sideways scroll at 375; 100% of text present with JavaScript off; in forced colours every button keeps a visible border. First load with fonts: J 93 kB in 3 requests, N 76 kB in 2 requests, no script. With all six scripts shown (fonts for Devanagari, Tamil, Arabic, Thai, Japanese load only when shown): about 320 to 340 kB. The Devanagari font alone is about 166 kB.
- **J's flaw found in the spike:** J's main button and accent button were the same indigo, so the two variants could not be told apart. Using J's deepest ink for main actions and the indigo for accent and links fixed it, and all pairs still passed.
- **Not tested yet:** a forced-colours look at whether either page still reads as its brand; right to left on a whole page (only individual buttons and the form were checked in Arabic); either page re-skinned into the two adopting sites' brand guidelines; any test with real people.

## Chairman's verdict

### Where the council agrees

- J, N and L are the top three for all five advisors, in different orders. Nothing else made anyone's list.
- L is a section, not a site: its identity belongs to the brands, not Saral.
- Eleven options fail on hard rules, not taste. D, O, F and C break one-accent or forced colours; H, M and B fail too many contrast pairs; K and E use ornament the owner ruled out; I copies a government system; A and G are stock templates with failing accents.
- No photographs, motifs, glass or gradients. Richness comes from one hue's depth and the range of scripts.
- J has the best palette: one indigo ramp, 9 of 9 pairs passing, with a designed dark mode, not an inverted one.

### Where the council clashes

- N or J as the lead. First Principles and the Contrarian lead with N: the house look is one theme among many, so identity must come from checkable proof. The Executor, Outsider and Expansionist lead with J: it feels finished and trustworthy, and N reads cold to designers. Both are right about different visitors. The First Principles argument is stronger: Saral's product is that the look is swappable, so a brand built on a look undercuts its own claim.
- The serif. Three advisors keep Newsreader as J's personality; the Contrarian drops it; the Executor keeps it with a plan to drop it if the budget breaks.
- Memorable or buildable. The Expansionist wants a signature people copy; the Executor wants what one maintainer can ship. The proof-led structure answers both: the datasheet home page is the thing others will copy.

### Blind spots the council caught

- Geist is Vercel's typeface, and Atkinson has no Devanagari, Tamil or Arabic. Only the Contrarian saw either.
- Numbers typed by hand go stale and read as hype. They must come from the build.
- Fidelity bias: I to N are rendered HTML, the rest AI pictures, and every top three came from the rendered set. The verdict holds anyway, because the losers lose on measured contrast and the rules, not on rendering.
- Forced colours removes the indigo, so the identity has to survive in type, spacing and structure alone. That favours N's structure.
- Right to left in N's dense table is unchecked on a whole page.
- The spike facts settle two arguments: both pages came in under budget (J 93 kB, N 76 kB), and they expose a gap nobody priced: the Devanagari font alone is about 166 kB, so a Hindi page breaks 150 kB even with no serif. The spike also found J's main and accent buttons identical; deepest ink for main actions fixed it.
- Saral has no mark for a favicon, README card or slide.

### The recommendation

**Spec sheet structure, Neel colour, one typeface.**

- Structure from N: strict grid, hairline rules, square frames, dense mono metadata, the home page as evidence.
- Colour from J: every shade from one indigo on cool white, with J's stated dark palette. Deepest ink for main actions; indigo for accent, links and focus.
- Type: Anek for everything, code in the system monospace. Anek covers Latin, Devanagari and Tamil. Hierarchy from weight and scale.
- Shape: controls keep J's soft corners so fields feel touchable; frames stay square.
- Motion: almost none.

Home page, top to bottom: the headline "Build it plain. Make it yours." with the install line; beside it, J's real form with a live error (this answers "cold"); N's component table generated from the specs (element, status, JS weight, works without JS, live preview); four numbers produced by the build; one band from L, the same form in two unrelated Saral themes with markup and theme file shown through `<details>`, no extra fonts and no consumer labels; J's row of one button in five scripts, low, with no caption.

Component page: the shared page generated from each spec (left navigation, live example, when to use, anatomy, states, API and tokens, accessibility, writing, dated changes).

Left out: any serif, Geist, a second accent, photographs, ornament, and in-house names like "Neel" or "llms.txt" on the site.

The serif is dropped. Newsreader is Latin only and Eczar stops at Devanagari, so the brand's character would vanish in Arabic, Tamil, Thai and Japanese, the scripts Saral claims to serve. A Hindi headline must carry the same weight as an English one. A serif also adds a second family to a budget Devanagari already breaks. Character comes from N's structure, which survives forced colours, and from the indigo's depth.

### The one thing to do first

Rebuild the spike as one home page in this combined style, and measure it in forced colours, full right-to-left Arabic and Hindi against the 150 kB budget, before any other page is designed.
